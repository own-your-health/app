Own Your HealthRecords

Privacy policy

Own Your Health is a web app that shows a household's medical records from a folder in the household's own Google Drive. A tool on the household's Mac, medrec, keeps that folder. This page says what the app reads, what it keeps, and what it sends.

No server

The app is a set of static files served by GitHub Pages. It has no server, no database and no account of its own. It runs in your browser, signs in to Google there, and reads your records folder from Google Drive there. Nobody who publishes the app can see your records, your folder or your sign-in.

What is sent, and where

Nothing from your records is sent anywhere but back to Google Drive. Apart from its own site (below), the page talks only to Google: the sign-in at accounts.google.com and oauth2.googleapis.com, and the Drive API at www.googleapis.com. Its Content-Security-Policy blocks every other host. It loads no analytics, advertising, fonts or other third-party scripts, and it sends no error reports.

Besides Google, the page asks one site: its own, GitHub Pages, where it is published. Each time the app comes back to the front, at most every five minutes, it asks for its own start page, to see whether a newer version was published. The request carries nothing from your records, no account and no cookies; like any page load, it tells GitHub the time and your network address.

A record's page can link to MedlinePlus (the National Library of Medicine's health site) or to the CDC's statement on a vaccine, to read about that test, medicine, condition or vaccine. The link carries the thing's code. When a record has no code those sites read, it carries the record's name instead, and the page shows those exact words beside the link before you tap it. It never carries your result, a date, your name or any id of yours or your records. The page never contacts those sites itself. A link opens only when you tap it, in a new tab, without telling the site which page you came from.

A paper's own file (a PDF, a picture, a letter) is read from your Drive when you tap View, and not before. It is shown in the page, by code that is part of the app, and held in memory until you close it or leave the page. Nothing inside a paper runs, and no address in it is contacted. Open in Drive opens it on Google's own page instead.

Download CSV, on the costs screen, makes the file in the page and saves it to your device. Nothing is uploaded.

What your browser keeps

  • The Google OAuth client id your household registered.
  • The id and name of the records folder you chose.
  • The email of the Google account that last signed in, so the next sign-in needs no account chooser. It goes to Google as a sign-in hint and nowhere else.
  • The colour theme, when you choose one in Settings.
  • The month your health plan's year starts, when you set it in Settings.

None of these is a secret. The Google sign-in itself lives in the page's memory only, and ends when the page closes or after an hour. Records read from the folder stay in memory and are gone when the page closes. Signing out forgets the account email; the browser's site settings remove the rest.

Google permissions, and why

  • See and download your Google Drive files (drive.readonly): to read the records folder medrec writes, which the app did not create.
  • See, edit, create and delete only the Drive files this app uses (drive.file): to write a small request into the folder's _requests/ folder when you ask the Mac to refresh or mark a bill paid, and to remove a request of its own when you cancel or undo it. A payment's request holds the ids of the person and the bill, the day and the amount.

The app changes nothing else in your Drive. It never asks for or holds a password to a provider's portal.

Google user data

The app's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data from your Drive is used only to show your records to you in your browser and to write the requests you ask for. It is not transferred to anyone, not used for advertising and not sold, and no one who publishes the app can read it.

Removing access

Sign out in the app's Settings to end the sign-in in this browser: the app forgets its token, which expires within the hour anyway. Signing out does not sign medrec out on the Mac. To remove the app's access to your Google account altogether, open your Google Account, then Security, then Third-party connections (Your connections to third-party apps and services), choose the app, and remove its access. Google removes it for every client of the same Google Cloud project, so if medrec on the Mac signs in to Drive through that project, it is signed out too, and the Mac loads no records until someone signs medrec in again. Your records folder stays in your Drive either way.

Contact

Questions about this policy or the app go to the project's GitHub issues. Please do not post records or personal details there. The source code is at github.com/own-your-health/app.
